What Software To Start Your New Solicitor Practice
By InCounsel App
Read time: 5 minutes
You want reliable software that does not become a headache after six months. Choosing the right tools in the first weeks shapes how you run files, bill clients and protect records. The wrong early choice creates migration costs, lost time and disruption to client service. This article explains how to prioritise needs, where switching hurts most, and how to pick software that can reasonably grow with a small practice.
What Software To Start Your New Solicitor Practice, Why early software choices matter
Starting small feels like the natural choice, but early software decisions compound quickly. A simple billing app or free document storage can look appealing at day one. Over time however, missing workflow features such as matter linking, deadlines, or secure client messaging force manual workarounds. Those workarounds add time to each case and increase the chance of record errors.
Switching systems later brings direct costs that are often underestimated: exporting and cleaning historical data, rebuilding templates, retraining staff or suppliers, and the inevitable downtime while a new system beds in. For a solo or micro-firm, that disruption often affects the first handful of high-value matters, so the real cost is both financial and reputational.
What to prioritise in month one
Prioritise features that remove everyday friction. At a minimum choose software that helps you: create and view client and matter records; track deadlines and case notes; store and version documents; and record invoices and payment status. Those four areas stop basic errors and preserve client history in a portable way.
Also think about the user experience: how fast is basic data entry, how simple are templates and how easy is it to find a client record? If you can test a short trial with a real file, do so. This reveals hidden time costs and whether the product supports the document drafting and client messaging you will rely on most.
The true cost of changing later
Data migration is the single most expensive part of switching systems. Historical documents, email threads and bespoke templates rarely export in a clean, ready-to-use format. You should budget time to map fields, reconcile client balances and test that deadlines and reminders transferred correctly. Those hours are usually billed or taken from chargeable work.
Retraining also matters. Even small changes in workflows slow a sole practitioner or small team. Expect a learning curve of several weeks for familiar tasks and up to a few months before productivity returns to normal. Factor in contingency for interrupted client service and additional admin during the transition.
Choosing tools that scale with you
Look for software that supports common solicitor tasks now and grows beyond them. Core capabilities to check include matter-level overviews, document versioning, deadline history, client records and basic invoicing visibility. These features reduce repeat work as caseloads increase and keep records organised when you add staff.
A practical example: some platforms offer an onboarding hub and AI-assisted document handling that speeds early intake and summarises uploaded documents. One such platform, InCounsel, is a Bubble-built AI operations and case-management platform for UK law firms. It includes features like client and case records, a Casehub for notes and deadlines, an Onboarding Hub, document upload with AI summarisation, and invoice status visible at matter level. Do not rely on automated outputs without solicitor review.
Practical buying checklist
Use a short checklist to compare options quickly. Ask the vendor for these specifics before committing: what export formats are provided, how are deadlines and notes exported, is document version history preserved, what training support is included, and what are ongoing licence and user costs? Test exports with a sample client file so you can judge practical effort.
Finally, keep the decision proportional. If most matters are straightforward, a simple case-management package with reliable document handling and clear invoicing may be the right choice. If you expect growth, pick software with clear export paths and scalable features. If you are unsure how this applies to you, getting advice specific to your circumstances is the safest next step.
