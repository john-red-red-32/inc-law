Client Portal Law Firm UK — Improve Legal Client Experience
By InCounsel App
Read time: 6 minutes
Client portals can transform how law firms communicate, show progress and reduce administrative friction. Yet many portals stay unused because they are not relevant or easy to access.
This article sets out practical, UK-focused steps to make portals genuinely useful for clients. It explains what makes clients log in, how to design feed-driven updates and how InCounsel can auto-feed updates, notes and summaries to keep engagement high.
Client Portal Law Firm UK — Improve Legal Client Experience, Design for the client’s goals, not the firm’s inbox
Clients log in when a portal helps them achieve a specific goal quickly. That might be checking the next step, viewing a bill, or approving a document. Start by mapping the client journey and list the three actions each client type needs most. Prioritise those actions in the interface and reduce distractions.
Keep language plain and outcome-focused. Label buttons with the benefit, for example “Approve settlement” rather than “Submit response”. Use clear progress indicators and minimise clicks. Small, frequent wins teach clients that the portal saves time and worry.
Make notifications helpful and human
Notifications drive return visits when they are timely and relevant. Use short, human copy that explains why the update matters. For example: “New draft of your tenancy agreement is ready to review — 2 minutes to check.” Avoid generic subject lines that clients skim and delete.
Offer predictable delivery options. Let clients choose email, SMS or in-portal alerts and set frequency. For higher-sensitivity matters, add secure message previews in the notification and a single-click path to the exact item referenced. This makes the portal feel like a useful assistant rather than another inbox.
Feed-driven updates: what to auto-send and why
Auto-fed updates keep clients informed without extra admin work. Send three core types: status updates (case stage changes), concise notes (key decisions or next steps) and short summaries after milestones. Each update should answer two questions: what happened, and what they need to do next.
InCounsel can auto-feed these items directly into the portal so clients see a live timeline. InCounsel is a UK-built workflow and voice-AI platform for law firms. Auto-feeds reduce lag, improve transparency and lower follow-up calls. Keep each entry short, date-stamped and linked to the document or task it references.
Summaries, notes and permissioned access
Clients read short summaries more often than long documents. After key events produce a 2–4 sentence summary that explains outcomes in plain English. Attach a clear next-step action and an estimated timeframe. This reduces uncertainty and improves perceived progress.
Control access thoughtfully. Use role-based permissions for joint matters and share only what each recipient needs. Audit trails should be visible so clients can see who made edits. For data protection, keep summaries non-sensitive and link to secure documents for full details.

Onboarding and measurement: sustain engagement
A strong launch and ongoing measurement keep a portal active. Onboard clients with a 60–90 second video and a short checklist tailored to the matter type. Offer assisted login the first time and include a ‘where to find help’ card in the portal.
Track three simple metrics: percentage of clients logging in within the first week, actions completed via the portal and reduction in phone/email follow-ups. Use these signals to iterate the content and notification rules. Small, data-led improvements compound into reliably higher engagement.
