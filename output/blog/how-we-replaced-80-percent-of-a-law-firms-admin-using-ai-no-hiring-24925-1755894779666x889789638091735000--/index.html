How We Replaced 80% of a Law Firm’s Admin Using AI — No Hiring
By InCounsel App
Read time: 5 minutes
Many small and medium UK firms feel swamped by admin. We tested a focused approach that cut nearly 80% of routine administrative work by automating onboarding, client calls, meeting notes and deadline tracking. This article explains what we automated, how we implemented change without lengthy training, how we managed risk and what the results looked like in practice. Expect clear steps, compliance flags and realistic timelines for an England and Wales practice.
How We Replaced 80% of a Law Firm’s Admin Using AI — No Hiring, Why automating routine admin matters now
Administrative tasks quietly erode fee-earning time and staff morale. Many firms still rely on email threads, manual forms and billing splits that demand repetitive human input. Automating those processes frees lawyers to focus on clients and strategy.
Our aim was practical: remove repetitive admin without changing how lawyers work. We concentrated on onboarding, client calls, meeting notes and deadline tracking. That made early wins visible within weeks and reduced the perceived risk of change.

Automating client onboarding and payments
We replaced manual intake forms and payment steps with a guided digital onboarding flow. The flow captured client data, verified identity where required and created a matter with pre-populated fields in the case management system. That cut repetitive entry and improved consistency.
Automated payment links and staged invoices reduced follow-ups. Because the onboarding forms validated key fields, teams saw fewer post-onboarding fixes. For firms in England and Wales, this approach reduces wasted time and lowers client friction when starting new matters.

Hands-free note-taking and call capture
Replacing manual note-taking with voice-assisted capture made the biggest immediate difference. Meetings and calls were recorded (with consent), transcribed and summarised into structured notes tied to the matter. That removed duplicated effort and made knowledge searchable for the whole team.
InCounsel is a UK-built workflow and voice-AI platform for law firms. We ensured consent protocols and quick edit flows so lawyers retained control over final notes. Those safeguards align with data-protection expectations and professional duties, while delivering fast, usable summaries.

Deadline tracking and task automation that actually works
We tied court dates, limitation periods and client tasks into an automated tracking system. Deadlines created alerts in the matter, routed tasks to the right person and logged follow-up actions. That cut missed deadlines and reduced firefighting.
Automation also handled routine reminders and status updates. By using template-driven tasks and conditional triggers, teams spent less time coordinating and more time progressing matters. The approach reduced admin backlogs while keeping senior oversight where it matters most.

Results, risks and next steps for UK firms
After deploying the automation suite, the firm measured an overall 70–80% reduction in time spent on defined administrative tasks. Fee-earners reported more billable hours and less context-switching. Admin staff moved to higher-value work such as client liaison and file quality checks.
Risk management mattered: we used clear consent, audit trails and human review steps to meet professional and data-protection duties. The Law Society’s practical guidance on generative AI remains a useful reference when setting governance and supervision policies. Firms should start small, measure outcomes and scale the automations that deliver the clearest time savings.
